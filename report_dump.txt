[Heading 3] ABSTRACT
[Normal] Access to timely clinical guidance in rural and underserved regions is often hindered by the absence of specialists, unreliable internet, and poorly digitized patient records. To address these barriers, this project presents MediBOT, a context-sensitive personalized medical platform that fuses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), deep-learning-driven analysis of chest radiographs, and OCR-based digitization of paper reports inside an offline-first Progressive Web Application. What distinguishes the system is its patient-context injection mechanism: demographic details, blood group, known allergies, currently prescribed medications, and recent vital signs are serialized into the prompt prior to retrieval and generation, allowing the model to produce medically tailored and contraindication-aware answers rather than generic chatbot replies. Architecturally, the platform is organized into three tiers ù a React-Vite-Tailwind PWA frontend with IndexedDB persistence, a FastAPI middleware responsible for validation and routing, and an ML backend that combines Llama-2 7B, FAISS, sentence-transformer embeddings, ResNet101, and EasyOCR. The proposed CNN component categorizes chest X-rays across five lung-disease classes with roughly 84% test accuracy, while the OCR pipeline recovers structured allergies, medications, and vitals from scanned or photographed documents. Taken together, the system illustrates how context-aware medical reasoning, offline-first persistence, and multi-modal AI can be unified into a clinically deployable support tool for resource-limited environments.

[Normal] INTRODUCTION
[Normal] 1.1 Introduction to Project
[Normal] In rural and underserved regions, healthcare delivery continues to suffer from limited specialist availability, slow diagnostic turnaround, fragmented medical records, and unreliable network connectivity. To compensate, patients often turn to generic web searches or mainstream chatbot services, yet these tools rarely account for clinically important factors such as known allergies, ongoing prescriptions, blood group, or recent vital measurements. Within a medical context, such generic guidance is not merely unhelpful ù it can be outright dangerous when contraindications or emergency indicators go unnoticed.
[Normal] To address these challenges, this project introduces MediBOT, a context-sensitive personalized medical platform that fuses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), deep-learning-driven analysis of chest radiographs, and OCR-based digitization of paper reports inside an offline-first Progressive Web Application. In contrast to conventional medical chatbots, MediBOT embeds patient-specific clinical details directly inside the RAG prompt, so that the language model can yield personalized and contraindication-sensitive responses. The system integrates textual reasoning with CNN-driven chest X-ray classification and OCR-assisted report structuring inside a unified platform, making it a comprehensive clinical decision support tool.
[Normal] 1.2 Problem Statement
[Normal] Healthcare access in resource-limited environments is severely constrained by the shortage of medical specialists, poor internet connectivity, and the prevalence of paper-based medical records. Existing medical chatbot systems provide generic responses that do not account for individual patient profiles including allergies, current medications, blood group, and vital signs. This creates a risk of delivering medically unsafe advice. Furthermore, diagnostic tools for chest radiography remain inaccessible in rural clinics that lack on-site radiologists. Paper-based reports are difficult to digitize and integrate into digital workflows. There is a critical need for a unified platform that can provide personalized medical question answering, automated chest X-ray analysis, and report digitization while remaining functional under intermittent or absent network connectivity.
[Normal] 1.3 Motivation
[Normal] The motivation for this project comes from the critical healthcare gaps that exist in underserved communities. Although chest radiography is among the most widely used modalities for screening pulmonary conditions such as pneumonia, tuberculosis, pneumothorax, and COVID-19, many rural clinics operate without on-site radiologists. Compounding this, paper-based reports remain prevalent, which turns clinical data extraction into a slow, manual, and error-prone exercise.
[Normal] Another key motivation is the limitation of existing medical AI systems that function as generic knowledge retrieval tools without considering the patientÆs specific medical context. When a system ignores known allergies or current medications while providing medical advice, it creates a genuine safety risk. This project aims to bridge this gap by developing a system that is not only medically knowledgeable but also patient-aware, ensuring that every response accounts for the individualÆs clinical profile. The offline-first design further ensures that the system remains functional in the very environments where it is needed most.
[Normal] 1.4 Sustainable Development Goal of the Project
[Normal] This project is aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3: Good Health and Well-Being, which aims to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages. By providing AI-powered clinical decision support that works offline and personalizes medical advice based on patient context, MediBOT directly addresses healthcare access barriers in rural and underserved communities. The systemÆs chest X-ray classification capability extends diagnostic reach to areas without radiologists, while the OCR-based report digitization modernizes paper-based healthcare workflows. Overall, the project contributes to making quality healthcare guidance more accessible, equitable, and reliable across resource-constrained environments.

[Normal] LITERATURE SURVEY
[Normal] 2.1  Overview of the Research Area
[Normal] Medical AI and clinical decision support systems have become rapidly growing research areas due to advancements in large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, and deep learning-based medical imaging. The emergence of models like GPT-4, Llama-2, and Med-PaLM has demonstrated that language models can encode significant clinical knowledge when properly grounded with domain-specific retrieval and prompting strategies.
[Normal] Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a key technique for reducing hallucinations in LLM-based systems by consulting relevant documents before generation. However, most general-purpose RAG deployments are knowledge-centric rather than patient-centric ù they retrieve textbook content effectively but rarely treat allergies, medication conflicts, or live vital signs as first-class inputs that shape the response. This shortcoming is particularly limiting for frontline healthcare applications.
[Normal] Within medical imaging, transfer learning applied to deep residual networks remains a robust baseline for classifying chest radiographs. Work such as CheXNet has shown that deep convolutional architectures can rival expert performance in thoracic disease detection. OCR-based digitization has also attracted attention for handling scanned medical documents, with tools like EasyOCR providing usable multilingual OCR. However, most existing pipelines fail to feed extracted data back into a personalized reasoning pipeline. Finally, offline-first healthcare software remains rare despite being essential for remote care settings.

[Normal] 2.2 Existing Models and Frameworks
[Normal] 1. Generic Medical Chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini):
[Normal] General-purpose LLMs used for medical queries. They provide broad medical knowledge but lack patient-specific context awareness, ignore allergies and medications, and require constant internet connectivity.
[Normal] 2. Med-PaLM / Med-PaLM 2:
[Normal] GoogleÆs medical LLM fine-tuned on medical QA datasets. Achieves high accuracy on medical benchmarks but is proprietary, cloud-dependent, and does not incorporate individual patient profiles.
[Normal] 3. CheXNet:
[Normal] A DenseNet-121 based model for chest X-ray classification achieving radiologist-level performance. However, it is limited to image-only inference and does not integrate with patient decision support workflows.
[Normal] 4. BioGPT:
[Normal] A domain-specific generative model trained on biomedical literature. Strong at biomedical text generation but not designed for interactive clinical decision support with patient context.
[Normal] 5. RAG-based Medical QA Systems:
[Normal] Systems combining retrieval with LLM generation for grounded medical answers. Most implementations are knowledge-centric and do not inject patient-specific clinical data into the retrieval or generation process.
[Normal] 6. Offline Health Apps:
[Normal] Mobile health applications with offline capabilities for basic health tracking. These typically lack AI-powered reasoning, medical image analysis, or personalized clinical guidance.

[Normal] 2.3 Limitations Identified from Literature Survey
[Normal] 1. Lack of Patient-Context Awareness: Most medical chatbot systems provide generic responses without considering individual patient profiles including allergies, medications, and vital signs.
[Normal] 2. Cloud Dependency: Nearly all existing medical AI systems require constant internet connectivity, making them unusable in rural and underserved areas with poor network access.
[Normal] 3. Siloed Functionality: Medical imaging, text-based QA, and report digitization typically exist as separate systems with no unified workflow.
[Normal] 4. Limited Personalization: Existing RAG systems retrieve medical knowledge effectively but do not use patient-specific data to condition the generation process.
[Normal] 5. No Safety Mechanisms: Most medical chatbots lack built-in safety checks for allergy warnings, drug interaction alerts, or emergency symptom detection.
[Normal] 6. Poor Report Digitization: Paper-based medical reports remain difficult to convert into structured digital formats, and most OCR pipelines stop at raw text extraction.
[Normal] 7. No Offline Data Persistence: Few healthcare AI applications implement offline data persistence, meaning diagnostic history, chat transcripts, and vital signs are lost during connectivity disruptions.
[Normal] 8. Black-Box Models: Many medical AI systems lack transparency in their reasoning process, making it difficult for healthcare professionals to trust or verify the outputs.
[Normal] 9. Limited Multi-Modal Integration: Systems that combine text reasoning, image analysis, and document processing within a single deployable platform remain rare.
[Normal] 10. Scalability Concerns: Many prototype systems are designed for demonstration only and lack the architectural robustness needed for real-world clinical deployment.
[Normal] 2.4 Research Objectives
[Normal] 1. To develop a context-aware personalized medical Retrieval-Augmented Generation system that conditions generation on both structured patient information and retrieved clinical literature.
[Normal] 2. To integrate textual reasoning with CNN-driven chest X-ray classification and OCR-assisted report structuring inside a unified platform.
[Normal] 3. To implement an offline-first PWA design leveraging IndexedDB together with service-worker caching to handle intermittent connectivity in resource-limited environments.

[Normal] ResNet101 Chest X-Ray Classifier:
[Normal] A pre-trained ResNet101 backbone was repurposed for five-class chest X-ray categorization by replacing the fully connected layer with Dropout(0.4) followed by Linear(2048, 5). Training followed a progressive unfreezing strategy with data augmentation including RandomRotation, RandomAffine, RandomResizedCrop, ColorJitter, and RandomHorizontalFlip. [Normal] OCR + LLM Report Extraction:
[Normal] Images of paper medical reports are processed through EasyOCR for text recognition, followed by Llama-2 7B with a zero-shot extraction prompt to produce structured JSON containing allergies, active medications, and vitals. This removes the need for hand-labeled extraction datasets while producing clinically usable structure.

[Normal] 4. Frontend û Patient Context Hook (Key Excerpt)
[Normal] import { db } from '../lib/dexie';

export async function getPatientContext(patientId: string) {
  const patient = await db.patients.get(patientId);
  const vitals = await db.vitals
    .where('patientId').equals(patientId)
    .last();
  return {
    age: patient?.age, gender: patient?.gender,
    bloodGroup: patient?.bloodGroup,
    allergies: patient?.allergies || [],
    medications: patient?.medications || [],
    vitals: vitals ? {
      heartRate: vitals.heartRate,
      bloodPressure: vitals.bloodPressure,
      oxygenSaturation: vitals.oxygenSaturation
    } : null
  };
}
